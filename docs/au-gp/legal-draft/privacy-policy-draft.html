1. About This Policy

GP LINK RECRUITMENT AUSTRALIA PTY LTD (“GP Link”, “we”, “us”, or “our”) is a recruitment service for general practice. We introduce General Practitioners to Australian medical practices.

This policy explains what personal information we collect, why, who sees it, where it is held, how long we keep it, and what you can ask us to do. It applies to:

  • GPs already registered and working in Australia who are looking for a different practice
  • GPs who trained overseas and want to work in Australia
  • Medical practices, and the people who deal with us on a practice’s behalf
  • Anyone who visits our website or sends us an enquiry

Three commitments sit at the centre of it:

  • We do not contact your current practice or employer, and we do not tell them you are speaking to us. [LEGAL REVIEW 8]
  • A practice sees your details only after you have agreed to be put forward to that practice. We ask each time, for each practice. [LEGAL REVIEW 9]
  • We do not sell your personal information.

2. The Laws We Follow

We handle personal information in line with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles. Where the data protection laws of the United Kingdom, the European Union or New Zealand apply to a doctor we work with, we aim to meet those as well. [LEGAL REVIEW 1]

3. What We Collect

A. From everyone who enquires or creates an account

  • Name, email address and phone number
  • The country where you trained, and the questions or notes you send us
  • How you found us (for example the advertisement or page you came from)
  • Bookings you make for a call with us, and whether the call took place
  • Messages between you and us by email and WhatsApp, and notes of calls

B. From doctors: identity

  • Full legal name and date of birth
  • A copy of an identity document (passport or driver’s licence)
  • Nationality and residency status

C. From doctors: professional details

  • CV, employment history and cover letter
  • Qualifications and certificates
  • Medical registration numbers and status with your regulator (for example the GMC, the Irish Medical Council, the Medical Council of New Zealand, or Ahpra)
  • What you are looking for: locations, sessions, billing model, special interests, and when you could start

D. From doctors already registered in Australia

  • Your Ahpra registration number, registration type and expiry date
  • Whether your registration carries any conditions, undertakings or notations, and what they say
  • Fellowship (for example FRACGP or FACRRM) and whether you are vocationally registered
  • Whether a restriction on where you may bill Medicare applies to you (the “19AB” moratorium) and, if so, until when
  • Your right to work in Australia: citizen, permanent resident, or visa holder. If you hold a visa, its subclass, its expiry date, and whether it is tied to a sponsoring employer
  • Any supervision requirement
  • Your professional indemnity insurer and the expiry date of your cover, and a copy of the certificate
  • Medicare provider number and PBS prescriber number, once you are placed and these are being arranged or transferred for the new practice
  • Your current suburb, state and postcode; how far you are willing to travel; your notice period
  • The name of your current practice, if you tell us, so that we do not approach it

E. From doctors moving to Australia

  • Documents for registration and verification (for example MRCGP, CCT, certificates of good standing, EPIC / AMC verification status)
  • Supervisor and referee details you give us
  • Visa information, including details of family members who will be included in a visa application [LEGAL REVIEW 20]
  • Your target arrival date and family circumstances relevant to where you want to live

F. Sensitive information

Some of what we collect may be sensitive or attract special rules: for example regulatory and professional conduct history, conditions on registration, criminal history checks where a placement requires one [OWNER TO CONFIRM 9: is this actually collected?], and government identifiers such as passport, provider and prescriber numbers. We collect these only where they are needed for the service you have asked for, and with your consent. [LEGAL REVIEW 2]

G. From practices

  • Practice name, address, billing model, and details of the roles you want to fill
  • Names, roles, email addresses and phone numbers of your contacts
  • Your signed Recruitment Services Agreement and the details in it
  • Interview availability, decisions on candidates, offers and contracts

We collect only what is reasonably necessary to provide our services.

4. How We Collect It

  • Directly from you: forms on our website, the GP Link app, and documents you upload or email to us
  • From your conversations with us by email, WhatsApp, phone and video call
  • From advertising platforms, when you fill in a form on Facebook or Instagram about GP Link
  • From booking tools, when you book a call with us
  • From public registers of medical practitioners (see section 7)
  • From practices, about interviews, offers and placements
  • From regulators, referees or migration agents, where you have asked us to deal with them

We do not collect information about you from your current practice or employer.

5. How We Use It

  • To respond to your enquiry and arrange a call
  • To work out which roles you can take up and are likely to suit you
  • To check your registration and credentials
  • To introduce you to a practice you have chosen, and arrange interviews, offers and contracts
  • To help with the steps before you start work (for example provider and prescriber numbers, indemnity, and for doctors moving to Australia, registration and visa steps)
  • To keep you updated by email, WhatsApp, phone or notifications in the app
  • To meet our obligations to practices under our agreements with them, including confirming a placement and its start date
  • To run, secure and improve our website and app, and to measure our advertising
  • To keep records we are required to keep

6. How We Contact You, and Calls with Our AI Voice Assistant

Email, WhatsApp and phone. When you enquire or create an account we contact you about your enquiry and our services using the details you gave us. You can ask us to stop at any time by replying to a message, using the unsubscribe link in an email, or writing to us. [LEGAL REVIEW 5]

WhatsApp. We send and receive WhatsApp messages through a business messaging provider (DoubleTick), which operates on the WhatsApp Business platform run by Meta. Your name, phone number and the messages themselves pass through and are stored by those providers. We keep a copy of messages you send us.

Booking a call. We use Calendly to book calls and Zoom to hold them. When you book, Calendly receives your name, email address, time zone, phone number and any note you add, and Zoom receives the details needed to run the meeting.

Our AI voice assistant. GP Link uses an AI voice assistant to phone some people who have enquired, to help them book a call with our team. [LEGAL REVIEW 3]

  • We call you with the AI voice assistant only if you have agreed to that. If you have not, only a person will call you.
  • The assistant tells you at the start of the call that it is an AI assistant and that the call is recorded.
  • Calls with the assistant are recorded and transcribed. [LEGAL REVIEW 4] We keep the recording, the transcript, a summary, and the notes the assistant takes (for example where you are in your training or registration, your timeframe, and what you asked).
  • The call is handled by a voice-AI provider (Retell AI), which uses other providers to carry the call (Twilio), to understand and generate the conversation (an AI model from Anthropic) and to produce the voice (ElevenLabs, with Cartesia as a back-up). To place the call the provider receives your name, phone number, email address, country, local time, and the question you asked us.
  • If you tell the assistant, or us, not to call again, we stop AI calls to you.
  • The assistant does not leave information with anyone else and does not call your practice.

At the date of this policy the AI voice assistant does not call Australian phone numbers. [OWNER TO CONFIRM 10: keep or delete this sentence depending on the decision about Australian calls.]

Video calls. For some calls, including interviews between a doctor and a practice, we switch on Zoom’s AI note-taker, which produces a written summary and action items. We do not record the video or audio of those calls. We store the summary and use it to follow up accurately. Tell us at the start of a call if you would prefer we did not use it. [LEGAL REVIEW 19]

7. Checking Your Registration

We check the registration details you give us against public registers. For a doctor registered in Australia this is the public Ahpra register. For doctors who trained elsewhere it is the register of the relevant regulator (for example the GMC, the Irish Medical Council or the Medical Council of New Zealand). We record the result and the date, and may repeat the check before you start a role. [LEGAL REVIEW 14]

We ask you to give us your Ahpra registration number, registration type and any conditions yourself, and we compare them with the register. If they differ, we ask you before relying on either.

8. AI Tools We Use on Documents and Messages

We use AI services, mainly from Anthropic, to help our team. [LEGAL REVIEW 7] They are used to:

  • Read and check documents you upload, including your identity document (to read the name, document type and expiry date), qualification certificates and certified copies
  • Read your CV, check it belongs to you, and help write the short introduction a practice receives
  • Suggest which roles may suit you, using your preferences and the notes from your call with us
  • Sort and summarise messages, emails and call notes, and help our team draft replies
  • Summarise video calls (through Zoom’s AI note-taker) and compare a contract with what was discussed

When one of these tools is used, the document or text is sent to the AI provider for processing.

A person decides. AI results help our team; they do not by themselves decide whether you are put forward for a role, whether your documents are accepted, or whether you are offered a position. AI results can be wrong. They are not legal, regulatory or professional verification of any document, and the regulator or the practice makes the final decision.

12. Cookies and Advertising Measurement

The GP Link app uses cookies to keep you signed in. Our public marketing pages also use two advertising measurement tools. Neither runs inside the signed-in app. [LEGAL REVIEW 10]

Meta Pixel. It sets cookies (such as _fbp) and shares page visits, enquiry and booking events, your IP address and browser information with Meta so we can measure our advertising and improve who sees it. We do not pass your name, email address or phone number to the pixel. [OWNER TO CONFIRM 15: confirm “automatic advanced matching” is off in Meta Events Manager, or this sentence is wrong.]

If you enquired through a form on Facebook or Instagram. So that Meta can tell which advertisements lead to real enquiries, we send Meta updates about that enquiry (for example that a call was booked, or that you created an account). Those updates include Meta’s own reference for your form and your email address and phone number in a scrambled (“hashed”) form that Meta can match to your account. We do this for up to 35 days after your enquiry.

Google tag. It sets cookies (such as _gcl_au) and shares page visits and booking events, your IP address and browser information with Google so we can measure our Google Ads advertising. It does not receive your name, email address or phone number from us.

You can limit this through your browser’s cookie settings, your Meta ad preferences at facebook.com/adpreferences, your Google ad settings at myadcenter.google.com, or industry opt-outs such as youradchoices.com.au.

13. Security

  • Information is encrypted in transit (HTTPS)
  • Uploaded documents are kept in private storage and opened through links that expire
  • Staff access depends on role, and support staff see only the doctors assigned to them (section 9)
  • We keep a log of staff actions

No system is completely secure. If a data breach is likely to cause you serious harm, we will tell you and the regulator as the law requires. [LEGAL REVIEW 13]

14. Your Choices and Rights

  • See what we hold. You can download a copy of your account information from the Account page, and ask us for anything else we hold, including call recordings and messages.
  • Correct it. You can update most details in the app, or ask us to.
  • Withdraw consent. You can withdraw your agreement to AI calls, to marketing messages, or to being put forward to a practice. If a practice already has your details we will tell it you have withdrawn and ask it to delete them.
  • Delete it. You can close your account or ask us to delete information, subject to what the law requires us to keep.
  • Deal with us without giving a detail. You can ask general questions without identifying yourself, but we cannot put you forward for a role without your identity and registration details.

We will respond within 30 days. [LEGAL REVIEW 12] Doctors in the UK or EU may have further rights under local law, including to object to some processing and to data portability.

15. Complaints

If you think we have mishandled your information, please contact us first using the details in section 18. We will acknowledge your complaint within [OWNER TO CONFIRM 2: days] and respond within 30 days.

If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au). Doctors in the UK can contact the Information Commissioner’s Office, and doctors in the EU or New Zealand their local privacy regulator.

16. Children

GP Link is for medical professionals and people over 18. We do not knowingly collect information from children, except details of family members that a doctor gives us for a visa application.

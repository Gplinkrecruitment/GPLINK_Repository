What you told us

  • You want to live in Perth's CBD and commute to work.
  • You are moving with your wife and no children, so a city apartment suits you well.
  • You want to earn well, through procedures, care plans and the occasional Saturday, while keeping a real work-life balance.
  • Your friend Dr Ben Darko works in Secret Harbour, 12 minutes from the clinic, which is nice to have nearby but not the deciding factor.
  • You are aiming for a June 2027 start.

The good news is that city living and a busy coastal practice fit together better than they look on a map. The Mandurah line leaves from under the CBD and reaches Warnbro, five minutes from the clinic, in about 40 minutes, and the drive runs against the peak traffic in both directions.

Why we recommend Waikiki GP Super Clinic

Of all the GP West practices, Waikiki is the one we would choose for you. It gives you the largest patient base to bill against from your first week, the procedural work you asked about, and a beach five minutes from the consulting room. The commute from the CBD is set out honestly below: it is a reverse commute with a train at each end, and the busier book more than pays for it.

  • A full book from day one. Dr Puttappa singled Waikiki out for its patient volume: walk-ins and overflow come to new GPs from the start, and the clinic's move to Medicare bulk billing on 1 November 2025 keeps the waiting room full. Your $150 an hour guarantee covers the first three months, and a busy book is what carries you to the $500,000 in annual billings within the first year as he described.
  • Built for procedures. Dedicated consulting rooms and modern treatment rooms, with pathology, physiotherapy, psychology and a skin check clinic under the same roof. The principal, Dr Gurvinder Saini, came from plastic surgery, so skin excisions and minor procedures are part of the daily mix alongside chronic disease and mental health plans.
  • UK colleagues already there. Dr Bruce Mahlangu and Dr Joshua Shields both trained in the UK, and Dr Puttappa worked in the UK himself before Australia. Your first weeks on Best Practice software and Medicare billing come with people who made the same move.
  • A reverse commute. You leave the city in the morning while everyone else is driving in, and come home against the outbound rush. The clinic is five minutes from Warnbro station and two minutes from the freeway, so both the train and the car work.
  • A swim before the train home. Warnbro Sound and the Rockingham foreshore are five to eight minutes from the clinic. A late-afternoon swim in summer, then the train back to the city, is an easy routine to keep.
  • Ben is 12 minutes from the clinic. Close enough for lunch or a drink after a Saturday session, even with you living in town.
  • Straightforward paperwork. Waikiki is a Distribution Priority Area, so your Medicare provider number as an overseas-trained GP is routine, and GP West's priority sponsor status speeds up the visa nomination.

Getting your bearings

The clinic sits at the Waikiki Village shopping centre on Read Street, 47 km south of the CBD down the Kwinana Freeway and the Mandurah rail line. From your front door in the city there are two ways to get there, and the map below shows how the other areas in this guide sit along the way.

  • The train. The Mandurah line runs from Perth Underground and Elizabeth Quay station every 15 minutes, more often at peak, and reaches Warnbro in about 40 minutes. The clinic is about three kilometres from the station, a short bus, bike or rideshare hop.
  • The car. The Kwinana Freeway runs straight from the city to Rockingham. Allow 30 to 35 minutes, driving against the peak flow in both directions.
  • Rockingham is the regional city at the clinic's end of the line: around 140,000 residents, its own centre and shopping precinct, a foreshore of cafes and bars, Rockingham General Hospital and a Murdoch University campus.
  • Secret Harbour, where Dr Darko works, is 12 minutes south of the clinic along the coast.

Perth is the top of the line and the clinic is 40 minutes down it. Cockburn Central and Fremantle are the halfway points, and Rockingham, Safety Bay and Secret Harbour cluster within 12 minutes of the clinic if you ever want a shorter commute.

Option 1: Perth CBD and Elizabeth Quay, our first pick

If you want to be in the middle of everything, this is it. The CBD's apartment towers sit between the Swan River and Kings Park, and Elizabeth Quay station, where your train to Waikiki leaves from, is at the bottom of the hill.

Why you will like it

  • Elizabeth Quay and Perth Underground stations are both on the Mandurah line, so the commute starts with a walk, not a drive.
  • Everything is on foot: the Hay Street and Murray Street malls, the restaurants along the quay and in the laneways, Kings Park at the top of the hill and the river at the bottom.
  • A pool, gym and secure parking are standard in the newer towers, and a two-bedroom apartment with city or river views is comfortable on the billings Dr Puttappa described.
  • The freeway on-ramp is five minutes away for the days you drive.

Worth knowing

  • The CBD is at its quietest on weekday evenings and Sundays. If you want a street that hums at night, look at the laneways near Elizabeth Quay or at Northbridge next door.
  • Most apartments here are unfurnished; furnished short-stays are plentiful for your first month.

A good fit if you want to walk to the train, the river and dinner, and keep the car for weekends.

Option 2: Northbridge

Northbridge is the city's entertainment quarter, just across the railway from the CBD: Chinatown, the bars and restaurants of James Street and William Street, the WA Museum Boola Bardip and the Art Gallery. Perth station is on its doorstep and Perth Underground is a five-minute walk.

Why you will like it

  • The liveliest nights in Perth, with the city's best concentration of restaurants, bars and small venues within a few blocks.
  • Apartments run a little cheaper than the CBD towers a street away.
  • The Perth Cultural Centre, with the museum, gallery and State Library, is your local park.
  • Five to eight minutes on foot to the Mandurah line.

Worth knowing

  • Friday and Saturday nights are loud on the main streets, so choose a side street or an upper floor.
  • Parking is tighter than in the CBD towers, so check the apartment comes with a bay.

A good fit if restaurants and nightlife on the doorstep matter as much as the commute.

Option 3: East Perth and Claisebrook Cove

East Perth is the quieter, riverside end of the city: townhouses and low-rise apartments around Claisebrook Cove, a 15-minute walk or one train stop from the CBD, with Optus Stadium across the Matagarup footbridge.

Why you will like it

  • Riverside paths, parks and the cove's cafes, with the stadium and the Swan River on your evening walk.
  • Quiet evenings and a village feel, with the CBD's restaurants a short walk or ride away.
  • Claisebrook station is one stop from Perth, where you change for the Mandurah line, or it is a 15-minute walk to Perth Underground.
  • Riverside Drive takes you onto the freeway in minutes on the days you drive.

Worth knowing

  • Fewer restaurants and shops than the CBD or Northbridge, so most evenings out start with a walk into town.
  • The train commute adds a change at Perth, worth five minutes.

A good fit if you want the river and the quiet with the city a walk away.

Option 4: South Perth and Como

South Perth sits directly across the river from the CBD, with the best view of the skyline in the city. The Mends Street ferry crosses to Elizabeth Quay in seven minutes, the Kwinana Freeway starts here, and Canning Bridge station on the Mandurah line is at the Como end.

Why you will like it

  • The shortest drive to the clinic of any city option: the freeway on-ramp is at your door, so around 30 minutes.
  • The ferry puts you in the CBD in seven minutes for dinner, and the foreshore path, Perth Zoo and the cafes on Mends Street and Angelo Street are all on foot.
  • Canning Bridge station, at the Como end, is on the Mandurah line, so the train to Warnbro is a few minutes shorter than from the CBD.
  • Apartments with uninterrupted river and skyline views at unit rents close to the CBD's.

Worth knowing

  • It is across the river rather than in the CBD itself, so late nights in town end with a ferry, a rideshare or a ten-minute drive.
  • The best river-view apartments go quickly, so be ready to apply on the day.

A good fit if you want the skyline view and the shortest commute in the same place.

If you ever want a shorter commute

You do not need to decide this now, but if after a year you would like the beach closer than the train, these are the places along the line that cut the journey without giving up Perth.

  • Rockingham foreshore. The regional city centre eight minutes from the clinic: apartments over cafes and bars on a sheltered swimming beach, Rockingham station 36 minutes from Perth, and rents well under the city's.
  • Cockburn Central and Coogee. The halfway point: a modern transit precinct 25 minutes from the clinic and 20 minutes by train to Perth, with Coogee Beach and the Port Coogee marina apartments ten minutes west.
  • Fremantle. The port city, with South Terrace's cafe strip, the markets and a real nightlife: 30 to 35 minutes to the clinic and 30 minutes to Perth on its own line.
  • Safety Bay and Secret Harbour. The coast around the clinic itself, with Point Peron and the Shoalwater marine park ten minutes away, and Secret Harbour, where Ben works, twelve.

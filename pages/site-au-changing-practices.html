Changing practices: notice periods and restraint clauses

What to look at in your current agreement before you agree a start date somewhere new, and who can answer the questions we cannot.

This is general information, not legal advice. Your own contract is what counts. Where a question depends on the wording of your agreement, we say so, and we say who can answer it.

Start with your own agreement

Before you look seriously at another practice, find the agreement you signed with the one you are at now. Two parts of it matter most: how much notice you have to give, and whether it says anything about where you may work afterwards. Agreements differ a great deal, so the only reliable answer is the one in yours.

Notice periods

Most agreements between a GP and a practice set out how much notice either side must give. Check yours before you agree a start date anywhere else.

If you are working with us, tell us your notice period early. It means the start date we propose to a practice is one you can actually keep, and nobody is asked to wait for a date that was never realistic.

You give notice when you are ready, not before. We do not ask you to resign in order to be introduced to a practice.

Restraint clauses

Some agreements include a restraint: a clause about where you may work, and for how long, after you leave. They vary a great deal from one agreement to the next.

If your agreement has one, read it carefully, and tell us what it says before you apply for a role nearby, so nobody is surprised later.

Getting advice

Whether a clause applies to you, and what it means in practice, is a legal question. We are not able to answer it. Your indemnity insurer, or a lawyer who works with medical contracts, can.

It is worth asking before you sign anything new, not after.

Patients and records

How you tell patients you are leaving, and what happens to their records, is usually covered by your current agreement and by the practice's own policies. Your practice manager is the person to ask, once you have given notice.

Keeping it confidential

Looking at other roles while you are working is normal, and it is your business.

  • We never contact your current practice without asking you first.
  • A practice sees your profile only after you agree to share it with that practice for that role.
  • If one of your referees works at your current practice, tell us, and we check with you before we get in touch with them.
  • If you would like us to hold off contacting anyone until a certain date, tell us and we note it on your file.
